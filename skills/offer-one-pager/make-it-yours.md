# Make it yours: offer one-pager

Your version, last changed 24 September 2026.

Change anything here. The skill follows these instructions every time it runs.

## 1. Who the page is for
A busy person who has never heard of you and will give it thirty seconds.
Why it is here: it keeps the page short and the headline plain.

## 2. The sections, in order
1. Who it is for, in one line
2. Headline in the customer's own words
3. The problem, in two lines
4. What changes for them (the outcome)
5. What they get: four things at most; the weekly recipe and its video count as one
6. The price, with a short honest note
7. The guarantee, only if the offer has one
8. One call to action
Why it is here: this is the order a stranger's questions come in: is this for me, do they get my problem, what do I get, what does it cost, what do I do next. Four things, not five, because five filled the A4 page to its edge.

## 3. Length limits
Headline: 12 words or fewer. Problem: two lines, about 35 words. Outcome: about 30 words. Each thing they get: a name of up to 5 words and one line of up to 15. Price note: 20 words. Guarantee: 30 words. Button: 6 words, or the reply line when there is no link. Line under the button: 12 words. The PDF ends with room for two more lines.
Why it is here: these limits fit one A4 page with room to spare and still read on a phone, so one new line (like "nothing to pay until week one opens") does not push it to a second page.

## 4. One call to action
One button, one next step. Everything else on the page leads to it. If there is no link yet, the button says "Reply to the message this came with".
Why it is here: a reader given two choices often picks neither.

## 5. The headline
Build it from the words customers use about their problem or the result they want, not the name of the product. Use a direct quote only if it is exact, credited and yours to use.
Why it is here: people stop for their own words, not for ours.

## 6. How the price reads
Show it exactly as the offer file has it. A price still being tested says so kindly, for example "Founding price, for the first 20 members. It may change after the pilot." No price yet: "To be decided".
Why it is here: honest prices build trust with the first people who buy.

## 7. Tone
Plain and warm, like the founder talking across a table. Short sentences. No hype, no exclamation marks, no "revolutionary" or "game-changing".
Why it is here: a stranger trusts a calm page more than a loud one.

## 8. The questions
Ask only what the second brain does not already answer, two or three at a time:
- Where will people see this page: sent after a chat, printed, or passed on by a friend?
- Which headline sounds most like your customers? (Offer three drafts.)
- Have you a real photo that shows the thing itself? (Optional.)
- Where should someone go to say yes? (Only if the offer file has no call to action.)
Why it is here: add or remove questions here, and the skill asks them.

## 9. What to leave out
Company history, how the product is built, long feature lists, more than five items, logos of anyone else, and any testimonial, number or result that is not in the second brain.
Why it is here: every extra line costs the reader a second they may not give.

## 10. Photo
None by default. Use a real photo of the thing itself if the founder has one: Priya's own windowsill photo from `growth/photos`. No generated image on this page, not even one captioned "Illustrative": the windowsill scene shows a harvest, and it pushed the PDF to two pages in week 7.
Why it is here: a reader takes a photo on a sales sheet as Priya's own plants, so only her real one goes on.
